# AteThat — maintained product requirements

Updated: 2026-10-16. Current scope/acceptance edition accompanying the historical PRD PDF. Detailed contracts remain in the system/database/API designs. Later owner decisions supersede original PDF examples.

## Product contract across phases

Core owns authorization, validation, provider normalization, decimal calculations and MongoDB access. Food input is proposed, reviewed and explicitly confirmed. Unknown nutrients are not zero. Confirmation preserves provenance and immutable nutrition; targets, schedules and Tracking Day context remain traceable. General references are not personalized medical advice.

## Phase 1 — accepted baseline

[v1.0.0](https://github.com/architsangal/AteThat/releases/tag/v1.0.0): authentication/recovery, profile/preferences, USDA/private foods, personal servings/meals, preview/confirm/correct logs, attributed general benchmarks, dashboard/history and basic health schedules/actions. Owner isolation, session safeguards, deletion grace/purge and accurate error/empty states are required.

Detailed food history compacts after 90 days into daily summaries under the existing rule, unrelated to future catalogue retention. Production uses Cloudflare Workers static assets, VPS API/Core and external MongoDB Atlas.

## Phase 2 — accepted and released

[v2.0.0](https://github.com/architsangal/AteThat/releases/tag/v2.0.0). See [verification](PHASE_2_STATUS.md).

### Nutrition and recipes

Expanded nutrient groups/charts preserve source completeness and units. Averages count known recorded days, not invented zero days. Historical accepted targets remain distinct from current comparisons. Recipes support preparation, yield, fractional servings and ingredient overrides requiring fresh review. Quantity edits invalidate confirmation; recipe edits cannot change older logs.

### Wellness and scheduling

Water/weight/activity support owned, version-guarded CRUD, canonical decimal conversion and saved Tracking Day context. The optional water goal is user-selected. Schedules support daily/weekly/monthly intervals, multiple times/month dates/end dates, future-date pause/resume, versions and adherence. Missing monthly dates are skipped, ended pauses do not resume implicitly, and past/today schedules cannot be rewritten.

### Acceptance and revised scope

Local builds/tests, Mongo-backed smoke checks, responsive browser review and authorized test-account production sanity completed. B6/B12 identifier rejection and paused-schedule null times found during QA were fixed with regression tests.

Notifications, offline/PWA, AI and recovery implementation were excluded; Terms disclose recovery limitations. Barcode is removed entirely. No additional Indian-food provider is required to close Phase 2. Macro-distribution/protein-consistency visualizations remain optional, not shipped. These decisions supersede the original Phase 2 PDF checklist.

## Phase 3 — planned requirements

### Agreed addition: local-first food discovery

Normal search uses the shared local USDA catalogue and the user's private foods, without automatic external search. Explicit USDA discovery allows 10 searches/user/day, atomically enforced in Core. Import bounded results deduplicated by USDA ID; same-name different IDs remain distinct. Partial search nutrition must not be treated as complete detail.

Reuse Atlas with a separate logical food database, no Redis. Store necessary normalized fields, provenance and usage metadata; retain until manual cleanup with no inactivity TTL. Proposed initial budget: 20 MB including indexes, subject to measurement. Global upstream accounting covers search/detail/refresh. Provider cooldown uses a generic temporary-unavailability message while local search works. Shared source edits become private foods or log-only overrides.

### Original roadmap: assisted input and portability

- Nutrition-label extraction recognizes nutrients, units and serving basis with editable review and uncertainty.
- Food-photo/natural-language input proposes foods, quantities and states; ask for clarification rather than fabricate precision.
- Voice transcribes into the same structured-intent/review pipeline, not a separate calculator.
- CSV/JSON exports cover owned food/nutrition history, meals/recipes, targets, wellness, health items/events and relevant profile. Use one Core path for UI/agents. Compacted history exports summaries, not reconstructed deleted detail.
- External agents/MCP call application services, never the database. Candidate capabilities include food search/detail/logging, daily/history reads, schedule/actions, goals/profile and exports; tool names/contracts remain to be designed.

### Safeguards and acceptance

Assisted output is proposed, never silently confirmed. All writes obey normal ownership, validation, version/idempotency and confirmation rules. Agents require explicit least-privilege read/write/export grants, revocation and minimum actor/action/resource/time/outcome/request-ID audit records without secrets or unnecessary health payloads. This minimum audit cannot wait for the broader Phase 4 platform.

Test catalogue concurrency/reset, duplicates/variants, ownership, provider cooldown, storage exhaustion, freshness failure, cleanup references and historical snapshots. Test assisted uncertainty/units/rejection; export ownership/bounds; agent scopes/revocation/auditable confirmation.

### Decisions before implementation

Approve quota boundary/accounting, freshness, storage-full UX, indexes and cleanup reference handling. Choose AI/transcription providers, budget, consent, upload limits and retention before external health-data processing. Define export formats/limits/download expiry and agent authentication/grants. See [detailed backlog](PHASE_3_BACKLOG.md). This document does not authorize paid services.

## Phase 4 and later — platform evolution

Durable worker/outbox only when needed; broader audit/operational analytics, notifications, offline/PWA, identity expansion and measured scaling. Bounded Phase 3 export/AI jobs may precede the complete worker platform. Recovery and regional food datasets require separate approval. Barcode remains removed.
